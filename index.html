THE IDEA

Keep the alternatives in action space.

ABSTRACT

Expressive actions. Reactive driving.

End-to-end driving often predicts waypoints and delegates final actuation to hand-crafted controllers. Direct prediction of throttle, steer and brake remains less explored, especially when several controls are plausible for the same observation.

We introduce the Action Diffusion Transformer (ADT), an anchor-free conditional diffusion model that learns a distribution over vehicle controls with a noise-prediction MSE objective. At each control cycle, ADT samples multiple candidates and uses Nearest Neighbour Matching (NNM) to execute the candidate most supported by the others.

Evaluations on Bench2Drive show that diffusion-generated alternatives and candidate selection work together: ADT achieves 77.90 Driving Score and 55.00% Success Rate at 19.2 ms latency on Bench2Drive220, using front and rear RGB cameras, ego speed and a high-level navigation command.

CONTRIBUTIONS

From one prediction to a choice of controls.

01

Multimodality at the control interface

Represent plausible alternatives directly in action space, without trajectory anchors or waypoint intermediates.

02

A diffusion model for direct driving

Condition an action denoiser on the visual scene, speed and command. Use a single-step horizon to update control from fresh observations.

03

Selection that preserves a sampled action

Use a parameter-free medoid rule to exploit candidate agreement, without extra supervision or retraining.

METHOD OVERVIEW

Observe → sample → select → act.

01

Encode the scene

A ResNet-34 extracts image features. Command and speed embeddings enrich the tokens before Transformer encoding and learned-query attention.

02

Generate K candidates

Independent Gaussian initialisations share the same observation condition. DDIM denoising converts each one into a continuous control candidate.

03

Execute the medoid

Sanitise the candidate controls, measure their component-wise L1 distances, and execute the candidate with the smallest average distance to all the others.

Nearest Neighbour Matchingk* = arg mink [ Σℓ ≠ k ‖ũk − ũℓ‖1 / (3(K − 1)) ]

Choose an actual sampled control. Reobserve and repeat at the next control cycle.

QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS

Why selecting beats averaging.

Distinct controls can be plausible.

The blue candidates can separate into throttle and brake modes. The horizontal axis is sanitised steer; the vertical axis is sanitised throttle minus brake.

Averages can fall between modes.

The paper explains that taking the mean or median can produce coasting controls between the throttle and brake modes. An aggregate control need not be a likely member of the candidate distribution.

NNM keeps an actual candidate.

NNM chooses a sampled control supported by nearby candidates. Isolated candidates receive larger average-distance scores. This preserves a supported action instead of blending incompatible alternatives.
